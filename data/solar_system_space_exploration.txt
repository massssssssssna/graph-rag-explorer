2. Celestial Bodies & Orbital Mechanics
The gravitational dynamics of our Solar System dictate orbital trajectories and exploration timelines. The following declarative statements define the primary celestial entities and their physical relationships. 

2.1 The Sun and Earth System
The Sun is a G-type main-sequence star at the center of our Solar System. Earth is a terrestrial planet. Earth orbits the Sun. Earth supports liquid water oceans and biological life. The gravitational pull of the Sun governs the orbital motion of Earth and all neighboring planetary bodies. 

2.2 The Moon and Lunar Dynamics
The Moon is Earth's only natural satellite. The Moon orbits Earth. Tidal forces between Earth and the Moon stabilize planetary spin axes. Robotic and crewed exploration missions frequently target the Moon as a proving ground for deep space habitation. 

2.3 Mars: The Red Planet
Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun. Mars orbits the Sun. Mars features geological remnants of ancient river deltas and lake beds. Scientific instruments on robotic rovers have discovered organic molecules and hydrated minerals on Mars. 

3. Space Exploration Organizations & Launch Vehicles
Modern space exploration relies on collaboration between government space agencies and commercial aerospace manufacturers to develop launch infrastructure and orbital vehicles. 

3.1 NASA (National Aeronautics and Space Administration)
NASA is the civil space agency of the United States federal government. NASA explores the Moon, Mars, and deep space environments. NASA funds and manages robotic planetary exploration programs. NASA collaborates with international partners to operate low Earth orbit infrastructure. 

3.2 SpaceX and Commercial Aerospace
SpaceX is a commercial aerospace manufacturer and space launch service provider. SpaceX develops orbital rocket systems. SpaceX launched_by commercial contracts to resupply orbital infrastructure. SpaceX manufactures reusable rocket engines and booster stages to reduce launch costs. 

3.3 Falcon 9 Launch Vehicle Architecture
The Falcon 9 is a partially reusable two-stage medium-lift launch vehicle. SpaceX develops the Falcon 9. The Falcon 9 is launched_by SpaceX from launch facilities in Florida and California. The Falcon 9 carries Crew Dragon spacecraft into low Earth orbit. The Falcon 9 carries commercial and scientific satellites into space. 

4. Mars Exploration & Robotic Missions
Robotic surface missions represent the forefront of astrobiology and geological analysis on planetary bodies beyond Earth. 

4.1 The Perseverance Rover Mission
The Perseverance Rover is an autonomous robotic planetary rover. NASA develops and manages the Perseverance Rover mission through the Jet Propulsion Laboratory. The Perseverance Rover was launched_by NASA in July 2020. The Perseverance Rover landed_on Mars inside Jezero Crater in February 2021. 

4.2 Scientific Payloads and Discoveries
The Perseverance Rover explores the Martian surface to search for signs of ancient microbial life. The Perseverance Rover carries seven primary scientific instruments, twenty-three cameras, and two microphones. The Perseverance Rover carries sample collection tubes for future Mars Sample Return missions. The Perseverance Rover carries the MOXIE experiment. The MOXIE experiment discovered that breathable oxygen can be extracted directly from the Martian carbon-dioxide atmosphere. 

5. The International Space Station & Orbital Infrastructure
Low Earth orbit serves as a microgravity laboratory supported by international agreements and commercial launch vehicles. 

The ISS (International Space Station) is a modular orbital space station. The ISS orbits Earth at an average altitude of approximately 400 kilometers. NASA operates and maintains the ISS in partnership with international space agencies. The Falcon 9 rocket frequently launches cargo and astronaut crews that visit the ISS. The ISS carries scientific microgravity experiments across human physiology and materials science.
